# Asia-Pacific Government AI Evaluation Frameworks (Singapore, Japan, South Korea, China) — status as of November 2026

Sourcing notes: Findings marked "(verified by direct fetch)" come from pages retrieved during this research session (November 11, 2026). Findings from law-firm notes, news articles, or search summaries are labeled as secondary. Chinese-language documents are cited via clearly labeled English translations (CSET) or summaries (SESEC); where only a translation was consulted, this is stated. ## Singapore: IMDA / AI Verify Foundation — AI Verify, Project Moonshot, Global AI Assurance Sandbox, Model AI Governance Framework for GenAI, LLM/agentic testing starter kits

### Takeaway
Singapore has the most operationally complete government AI evaluation stack in the region: a governance-principles testing framework (AI Verify) with open-source toolkits for traditional AI and LLMs (AI Verify Toolkit, Project Moonshot), voluntary testing methodology guides (Starter Kits for LLM apps, Jan 2026, and agentic systems, Oct 2026), a live real-world testing marketplace (Global AI Assurance Sandbox), and a third-party tester accreditation scheme (AI TAP) — all voluntary, published by IMDA and its AI Verify Foundation.

### Cited Findings

**AI Verify testing framework and toolkit**
- AI Verify is a testing framework that assesses how responsibly an organisation implements an AI system against 11 internationally recognised AI governance principles: Transparency, Explainability, Reproducibility, Safety, Security, Robustness, Fairness, Data Governance, Accountability, Human Agency & Oversight, and Inclusive Growth, Societal and Environmental Well-being (verified by direct fetch) — [AI Verify Testing Framework](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/tools/ai-verify-testing-framework/)
- The framework is structured as four elements — principles, outcomes, processes, evidence. Process checks are validated primarily through documentary evidence (e.g., policy documents); for each check the organisation records evidence type, the evidence, and implementation status (Yes/No/N/A) (verified by direct fetch) — [AI Verify Testing Framework](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/tools/ai-verify-testing-framework/)
- Technical testing is split across tools: the AI Verify Toolkit does technical testing for traditional AI; Project Moonshot does technical testing for generative AI; a Process Checks Tool (generative AI) documents practices and generates a summary alignment report (verified by direct fetch) — [AI Verify Testing Framework](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/tools/ai-verify-testing-framework/)
- Key dates: launched 25 May 2022 as a Minimum Viable Product for traditional AI by IMDA and PDPC; framework and toolkit open-sourced on GitHub 7 June 2023; updated framework released 29 May 2025, extended to address generative AI risks (verified by direct fetch) — [AI Verify Testing Framework](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/tools/ai-verify-testing-framework/)
- The framework is crosswalked to the US NIST AI RMF (13 Oct 2023), NIST AI RMF Generative AI Profile (29 May 2025), the Hiroshima Process International Code of Conduct for Advanced AI Systems (29 May 2025), and ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (21 July 2025) (verified by direct fetch) — [AI Verify Testing Framework](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/tools/ai-verify-testing-framework/)

**Project Moonshot**
- Project Moonshot is an open-source testing toolkit that assesses the safety and reliability of LLMs and LLM-based applications through benchmark testing and red teaming; it implements the benchmarks recommended in IMDA's Starter Kit "out of the box," and is complemented by the AI Verify Process Checks Tool (verified by direct fetch) — [Project Moonshot page](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/tools/moonshot/); code at [github.com/aiverify-foundation/moonshot](https://github.com/aiverify-foundation/moonshot)
- Moonshot was launched in open beta on 31 May 2024 at Asia Tech x Singapore, combining red-teaming, benchmarking, and baseline testing, with results designed to be readable by non-technical users (secondary: law-firm client notes) — [Rajah & Tann](https://rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/singapore-launches-project-moonshot-generative-ai-testing-toolkit-to-address-security-and-safety-challenges); [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/28286/launches-generative-ai-testing-toolkit-project-moonshot-to-address-llm-safety-and-security-challenges)

**Starter Kit for Testing LLM-Based Applications for Safety and Reliability**
- The Starter Kit is a set of voluntary guidelines consolidating emerging best practices and methodologies for testing LLM-based applications; the Version 1.0 document is dated January 2026 (secondary: search-result reading of the official PDF; the PDF itself is the primary source) — [IMDA Starter Kit PDF](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/about/emerging-tech-and-research/artificial-intelligence/starter-kit-for-testing-llm-based-applications-for-safety-and-reliability.pdf)
- The Sandbox page (verified by direct fetch) confirms the Starter Kit supports testing for five risk areas: hallucination and inaccuracy; bias in decision-making; undesirable content; data leakage; vulnerability to adversarial prompts — [Global AI Assurance Sandbox](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/ai-assurance/sandbox/)

**Starter Kit for Testing Agentic Systems for Safety and Reliability (new, Oct 2026)**
- IMDA announced the agentic Starter Kit on 14 October 2026 at the "AI Assurance Exchange: The Agentic Edition" (organised by AI Verify Foundation and SGTech). It gives a four-step approach: (1) identify risks (e.g., incorrect actions, behaviour beyond permitted scope); (2) design realistic, robust tests; (3) conduct tests and analyse logs; (4) monitor and re-test continuously. It tests both the agent's reasoning and its actions using agent-specific metrics such as tool-use and handoff accuracy and human-in-the-loop rates (secondary: law-firm note) — [Allen & Gledhill, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/34742/imda-introduces-new-ai-testing-and-cybersecurity-initiatives)
- The agentic kit builds on the Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI and expands the earlier LLM-app Starter Kit; it draws on consultations with government agencies and 20+ industry deployers/testers, plus testing experience from the Global AI Assurance Sandbox and the Singapore AI Safety Institute (secondary) — [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/34742/imda-introduces-new-ai-testing-and-cybersecurity-initiatives)

**Global AI Assurance Pilot / Sandbox**
- The Global AI Assurance Sandbox, run by IMDA and the AI Verify Foundation, is an ongoing initiative pairing organisations deploying GenAI or agentic AI applications with specialist technical testing firms. Process: express interest → qualification → introduction to a testing partner (commercial terms between parties) → technical testing completed within three months. Deployers can receive some funding for specialist expertise (verified by direct fetch) — [Sandbox page](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/ai-assurance/sandbox/)
- The Sandbox builds on a 2025 Pilot Phase (the "Global AI Assurance Pilot"); outputs include published case study reports, a 2025 Pilot Main Report, and a Case Study Compendium 2026. As of the 2026 page: 30 applications across 12 geographies and 14 industries (verified by direct fetch) — [Sandbox page](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/ai-assurance/sandbox/); pilot/report pages at [/ai-assurance/2025-pilot-phase/](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/ai-assurance/2025-pilot-phase/) and [/ai-assurance/2025-main-report/](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/ai-assurance/2025-main-report/)
- The Sandbox does not provide a software testing environment or regulatory approval; it uses the Starter Kit as the starting point for what/how to test, and encourages use-case-specific risks (safety, health, financial, reputational impacts; insufficient human oversight; regulatory breaches) (verified by direct fetch + search summary of sandbox materials) — [Sandbox page](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/ai-assurance/sandbox/)

**Model AI Governance Framework for Generative AI**
- Published by AI Verify Foundation and IMDA; proposed draft released for international feedback 16 January 2024; final framework released 30 May 2024 (the page's PDF filename shows 19 June 2024 — minor inconsistency on the page itself) (verified by direct fetch) — [MGF for GenAI](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/resources/mgf-gen-ai/)
- Its nine dimensions: 1. Accountability; 2. Data; 3. Trusted Development and Deployment; 4. Incident Reporting; 5. Testing and Assurance; 6. Security; 7. Content Provenance; 8. Safety and Alignment R&D; 9. AI for Public Good (verified by direct fetch) — [MGF for GenAI](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/resources/mgf-gen-ai/)
- Dimension 5 (Testing and Assurance) calls for external validation through third-party testing and the development of common AI testing standards; Dimension 4 calls for incident management systems for timely notification and remediation (verified by direct fetch) — [MGF for GenAI](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/resources/mgf-gen-ai/)

**AI Tester Accreditation Programme (AI TAP) and related**
- AI TAP, launched by the AI Verify Foundation, accredits third-party AI testing service providers across sectors (healthcare, finance, recruitment, public services); five firms accredited as of the 14 Oct 2026 announcement (secondary) — [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/34742/imda-introduces-new-ai-testing-and-cybersecurity-initiatives); programme page [/ai-assurance/tester-accreditation/](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/ai-assurance/tester-accreditation/)
- The AI Verify Foundation homepage also lists a Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI (PDF hosted at imda.gov.sg) and Agentic Testing Guidelines as resources (verified by direct fetch of homepage; dates not stated there) — [AI Verify Foundation](https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/)

### Inferences
- Singapore's approach is a deliberately layered voluntary stack: governance framework (what good looks like) → testing framework (process checks + technical tests) → open-source toolkits (how to run tests) → starter kits (test methodology for LLM/agentic apps) → sandbox (real-world deployer-tester pairings producing public case studies) → accreditation (professionalising the tester market). This makes it the clearest worked example of a government building an AI assurance *ecosystem* rather than a compliance regime.
- The explicit crosswalks (NIST AI RMF, Hiroshima Code of Conduct, ISO/IEC 42001) show Singapore positioning AI Verify as interoperable evidence for multiple regimes — useful for teaching how evaluation artifacts can be reused across frameworks.

### Gaps
- Exact current version/release status of Project Moonshot and the AI Verify Toolkit are not stated on the official pages (no version numbers found).
- Release date of the Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI and the Agentic Testing Guidelines could not be verified (the foundation homepage lists them without dates; the agentic Starter Kit announcement implies around Oct 2026 but does not date the MGF itself).
- Whether Starter Kit v1.0 (January 2026) formally supersedes the earlier 2025 draft version announced at ATxSG May 2025 was not verified against the PDF text.
- The Singapore AI Safety Institute is referenced as a contributor to the agentic Starter Kit, but its own publications were not researched here.

## Japan: AI Safety Institute evaluation guides, AI Guidelines for Business, and the AI Act

### Takeaway
Japan's AI Safety Institute (hosted at IPA since 14 February 2024) publishes the region's most methodologically explicit evaluation guides — the Guide to Evaluation Perspectives on AI Safety (now v1.20, July 2026) and the Guide to Red Teaming Methodology on AI Safety (v1.10, March 2025) — plus sector guides (healthcare, AI robotics), a data quality guidebook, and an open-source evaluation tool; all sit within a soft-law regime anchored by the voluntary METI/MIC AI Guidelines for Business and a penalty-free 2025 AI promotion Act.

### Cited Findings

**Japan AI Safety Institute (J-AISI)**
- J-AISI was launched 14 February 2024 (announced by PM Kishida 21 December 2023), with its secretariat at the Information-technology Promotion Agency (IPA); under the Integrated Innovation Strategy 2024 it is established at IPA as the central body for AI safety. Mandate: researching safety evaluation and drafting related standards, guidance, and test environments; studying evaluation methods with industry/academia; international coordination with UK and US AISIs (verified by direct fetch) — [AISI About page](https://aisi.go.jp/about/)
- The AISI site's "EN" button is machine translation (Google Translate), not an official English site; English PDFs of key guides are published separately (verified by direct fetch) — [aisi.go.jp](https://aisi.go.jp/)

**Guide to Evaluation Perspectives on AI Safety**
- Version history (from the official output listing, verified by direct fetch of [aisi.go.jp/output/](https://aisi.go.jp/output/)): first published 18 September 2024; v1.10 published 28 March 2025; v1.20 (Japanese) published 7 July 2026 ([announcement page](https://aisi.go.jp/output/output_information/260707/)). Permanent landing page: [Guide to Evaluation Perspectives on AI Safety](https://aisi.go.jp/output/output_framework/guide_to_evaluation_perspective_on_ai_safety/)
- English PDF of v1.10 (dated 28 March 2025): [ai_safety_eval_v1.10_en.pdf](https://aisi.go.jp/assets/pdf/ai_safety_eval_v1.10_en.pdf). Per a third-party summary, it translates six key elements of AI safety into ten evaluation perspectives, including control of toxic output, privacy protection, security, and robustness; it refers red-teaming methodology to the separate guide (secondary summary — exact perspective list should be confirmed against the PDF) — [AI Governance Library summary](https://www.aigl.blog/guide-to-evaluation-perspectives-on-ai-safety/)
- AISI's own slide deck describes the guides as supporting AI safety evaluation and testing for businesses that develop, provide, or use AI (official PDF) — [AISI presentation, 26 Mar 2025](https://aisi.go.jp/assets/pdf/20250326_powerpoint_en.pdf)

**Guide to Red Teaming Methodology on AI Safety**
- v1.00 published 25 September 2024; v1.10 published 31 March 2025 (verified by direct fetch of [aisi.go.jp/output/](https://aisi.go.jp/output/)). Permanent landing page: [Guide to Red Teaming Methodology on AI Safety](https://aisi.go.jp/output/output_framework/guide_to_red_teaming_methodology_on_ai_safety/)
- English PDFs: [v1.00](https://aisi.go.jp/assets/pdf/ai_safety_RT_v1.00_en.pdf) and [v1.10](https://aisi.go.jp/assets/pdf/E1_ai_safety_RT_v1.10_en.pdf). v1.00 covers the purpose of red teaming, typical attack methods on LLM systems, and timing/procedure before and after release; v1.10 adds sections on red team structure and roles and types of red teaming (search-result reading of the PDFs' tables of contents) — [search-indexed PDF contents](https://aisi.go.jp/assets/pdf/E1_ai_safety_RT_v1.10_en.pdf)

**Sector and supporting evaluation outputs (all from the official output listing, verified by direct fetch of [aisi.go.jp/output/](https://aisi.go.jp/output/))**
- Guide to Evaluation Perspectives on AI Safety in the Healthcare Sector, v1.0, published 2 April 2026 ([announcement](https://aisi.go.jp/output/output_information/260402/))
- AI Robotics safety evaluation perspectives guide (AIロボティクスに関するセーフティ評価観点ガイド), v1.0, published 23 July 2026 ([announcement](https://aisi.go.jp/output/output_information/260723/))
- Data Quality Management Guidebook: draft 7 February 2025, published 31 March 2025, updated 14 May 2026 ([landing page](https://aisi.go.jp/output/output_framework/data_quality_management_guidebook/))
- "AI Safety Evaluation Tool Released as Open Source Software (OSS)", 12 September 2025 ([announcement](https://aisi.go.jp/output/output_information/250912/))
- "Known Attacks and Their Impacts on AI Systems", V2 ([landing page](https://aisi.go.jp/output/output_security/known_attacks_and_impacts/))
- National Status Report on AI Safety in Japan 2024 (7 Feb 2025) and 2025 (28 April 2026); FY2025 Conformity Assessment Sub-Working Group activity report (23 April 2026); AI safety evaluation environment task force report FY2025 (15 April 2026) — all listed on [aisi.go.jp/output/](https://aisi.go.jp/output/)

**AI Guidelines for Business (METI/MIC)**
- The AI Guidelines for Business were released by MIC and METI on 19 April 2024 as an integrated revision of earlier guidelines; version 1.1 (March 2025) organises guidance around three actor categories — AI developers, AI providers, and business users — and is described as the most detailed articulation of government expectations (secondary: law-firm and policy analyses) — [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/06/aq-japans-ai-act-a-soft-law-approach-to-promoting-ai-development-and-utilisation); [NBR](https://www.nbr.org/?p=231431); v1.1 publication on 28 March 2025 confirmed on the official AISI output listing ([aisi.go.jp/output/](https://aisi.go.jp/output/), item "AI事業者ガイドライン(1.1版)", 2025-03-28)
- AISI published official crosswalks between the AI Guidelines for Business and the US NIST AI RMF (Crosswalk 1, 30 April 2024; Crosswalk 2, 18 September 2024), plus a Japanese translation of NIST AI RMF (4 July 2024) (verified by direct fetch of [aisi.go.jp/output/](https://aisi.go.jp/output/))
- A reported v1.2 of the Guidelines (31 March 2026, said to add AI agents and physical AI as explicit targets) rests on a single blog source and is NOT verified — [timewell.jp blog](https://timewell.jp/en/columns/japan-ai-promotion-act-business-guidelines-v1-2)

**Japan's AI Act**
- The Act on the Promotion of Research, Development, and Utilization of Artificial Intelligence-Related Technologies was passed by the National Diet on 28 May 2025. It is promotion-oriented soft law: no direct penalties; the main business duty is to cooperate with national/local government measures. It empowers ministries to request information from operators, analyze misuse cases affecting rights and interests, and conduct research supporting safe development and use (secondary: law-firm analyses) — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/japans-first-ai-legislation-becomes-law-focus-promoting-research-and-development-no); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/06/aq-japans-ai-act-a-soft-law-approach-to-promoting-ai-development-and-utilisation)
- Sources conflict on effective dates: one account says promulgated and partially enforced 4 June 2025 with full effect 11 September 2025; another says effective 1 September 2025. Not resolved in this session — [regulations.ai breakdown](https://regulations.ai/breakdowns/japan-ai-rd-act); [Nemko Digital](https://digital.nemko.com/regulations/ai-regulation-japan)
- One source says the AI Strategy Headquarters published "Guidelines for Ensuring the Appropriateness of Research & Development and Utilization of AI-Related Technology" on 19 December 2025 under the Act's guideline provision; sources differ and this was not verified against an official text — [Ohebashi newsletter](https://www.ohebashi.com/jp/newsletter/NL_en_2025autumn_Gujima.pdf); [IBA](https://www.ibanet.org/japan-emerging-framework-ai-legislation-guidelines)

### Inferences
- Japan is the clearest case of an AISI as the national evaluation methodology publisher: its guides tell businesses *how* to evaluate (perspectives, red-team structure, timing), while the law itself imposes no testing mandate. For a site teaching evaluation practice, J-AISI's guides are primary methodological sources rather than compliance documents.
- The 2026 sector guides (healthcare, robotics) and the conformity-assessment working group suggest Japan is moving from generic model evaluation toward domain-specific evaluation perspectives and eventual conformity-assessment infrastructure.

### Gaps
- Whether English translations exist for the v1.20 Evaluation Perspectives guide (July 2026), the healthcare and robotics guides, and the September 2025 OSS evaluation tool was not verified; the versions confirmed in English are Evaluation Perspectives v1.10 and Red Teaming v1.00/v1.10.
- The exact ten evaluation perspectives in the Evaluation Perspectives guide were not confirmed against the official PDF (only via a third-party summary).
- The AI Act's precise effective date(s) conflict across sources (see above).
- The name/URL of the OSS evaluation tool released 12 Sept 2025 was not captured.

## South Korea: AI Basic Act requirements for high-impact AI, Korea AISI, and TTA testing/certification

### Takeaway
South Korea's AI Basic Act (effective 22 January 2026, administered by MSIT) is Asia's first comprehensive binding AI law: high-impact AI operators must self-assess classification, document trust/safety measures, ensure human oversight and explainability, and make efforts at fundamental-rights impact assessment, while "high-performance" AI above 10^26 cumulative training FLOPs faces lifecycle risk-management and reporting duties; the Korea AI Safety Institute (launched under ETRI, Nov 2024) conducts model safety evaluations (42 models by Aug 2026), and TTA is building a private testing/certification ecosystem (IEEE CertifAIEd accreditation, AssurAI dataset).

### Cited Findings

**AI Basic Act (Framework Act on AI)**
- The AI Basic Act took effect 22 January 2026; MSIT (Ministry of Science and ICT) finalizes enforcement decrees with technical compliance details (verified by direct fetch of a law-firm analysis; the Act's official English translation was not located) — [Cooley, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways)
- High-impact AI is defined as AI that significantly affects human life, safety, or fundamental rights, including healthcare, energy, transportation, hiring, and biometric analysis applications; operators must assess before deployment whether their AI is high-impact and can request an MSIT determination (secondary) — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways)
- High-impact obligations per Cooley: document actions taken to secure trust and safety; create and deploy a user protection plan; maintain a mechanism for human intervention and oversight; provide meaningful explanation of outcomes plus a summary of training data; "make efforts" to assess impacts on fundamental rights before incorporating the AI (best-efforts duty) — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways)
- Sources disagree on the impact assessment's bindingness: described variously as a best-efforts duty and as a precondition for market placement (conflict noted, not resolved) — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways) vs. [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/south-koreas-new-ai-framework-act/)
- High-performance AI: per MSIT's legislative notice on the Enforcement Decree, systems trained with cumulative compute of at least 10^26 FLOPs; operators may be required to run a lifecycle risk management plan and user protection measures and report outcomes to MSIT (secondary) — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways)
- Generative AI: operators must notify users and label AI-generated content; clear notice required when AI-generated sound/image/video is hard to distinguish from human-created content (secondary) — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways)
- Enforcement: fines up to KRW 30 million (~US$21,000) for limited violations (failure to notify of AI use, no domestic representative, violating corrective orders, refusing inspections); MSIT can suspend services posing safety threats; MSIT signaled a one-year grace period on administrative fines. Duties like high-impact risk management plans and impact assessments carry no direct fine and escalate only via guidance and corrective orders (secondary) — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways)
- Decree timeline: MSIT released a draft Enforcement Decree 8 September 2025 and opened a 40-day public comment on a revised draft announced 12 November 2025 (comment until 22 December per one report); the decree took effect with the Act on 22 January 2026; the National Information Society Agency (NIA) published a suite of enforcement guidance documents on 22 January 2026, including practical guidelines for operators on high-impact AI (secondary: multiple sources via search) — [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/korea-releases-draft-enforcement-decree-of-the-ai-framework-act); [Asia Economy (English)](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2025111211021678692); [BKL](https://www.bkl.co.kr/en/law/insight/newsletter/6235)
- A news report dated 20 July 2026 describes a "Revised AI Basic Act" taking effect that day, with mandatory generative-AI labeling beginning "in earnest" and the amendment detailing generative AI labeling obligations and the management system for high-impact AI (secondary, single news source; amendment text not verified) — [Asia Economy (English), 20 Jul 2026](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026072018350629507)

**Korea AI Safety Institute (AISI)**
- The Act provides for an AI Safety (Research) Institute tasked with evaluating AI risk and developing standards; Cooley notes no certification scheme or formal testing regime in the Act itself (secondary) — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways)
- The Korea AI Safety Institute was launched in November 2024 as Korea's AI safety research hub under ETRI (Electronics and Telecommunications Research Institute) (secondary: research newswire) — [Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/ai-safety-institute-launched-as-korea-s-ai-research-hub)
- The institute's official English page describes its main tasks as risk identification, safety framework development, safety evaluation, and building evaluation infrastructure, including AI safety evaluation datasets and evaluation tools (from search snippets of the official page; direct fetch failed — connection refused on 11 Nov 2026) — [Korea AISI, AI Safety Assessment](https://www.aisi.re.kr/eng/sub/30); [Korea AISI English home](https://www.aisi.re.kr/eng)
- An August 2026 Korean news report (citing MSIT) says the institute had evaluated 42 domestic and international AI models to date, with no cases of pre-release evaluation; it also says the institute publicly released evaluation results for Kakao's Kanana model — while a 2025 interview with the institute's director had said evaluation results would not be disclosed externally (conflicting accounts noted) — [Money Today (English), Aug 2026](https://www.mt.co.kr/en/tech/2026/08/07/2026080614255237204); [Asia Economy interview, Jan 2025](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2025011313204556966)

**TTA (Telecommunications Technology Association) testing/certification**
- In January 2026 TTA signed an Accredited Assessment Collaborator (AAC) agreement with IEEE SA for the IEEE CertifAIEd program, enabling TTA to offer AI ethics assessment services; TTA also operates an "AI Trustworthiness Alliance" promoting a private ecosystem for trustworthy-AI verification and certification (secondary: Korean news, in Korean) — [Dailian](https://www.dailian.co.kr/news/view/1604525)
- TTA, with KAIST and other universities, Kakao, and the Korea AISI, built "AssurAI," a dataset for assessing AI risks, and ran a pilot evaluation with Kakao whose report is public (secondary: LinkedIn post by a participant — treat with caution) — [LinkedIn](https://kr.linkedin.com/in/sooyoungkim12)

### Inferences
- Korea pairs a binding law with mostly process obligations (documentation, oversight, self-classification) rather than prescribed technical tests; the actual evaluation capability sits in the AISI (model evaluations, datasets, tools) and TTA (third-party assessment market). For the Atlas, Korea illustrates the gap between legal "safety assurance" duties and concrete evaluation methodology, which guidance (NIA guidelines) is still filling.
- The 10^26 FLOPs threshold mirrors compute-based frontier definitions elsewhere (EU AI Act GPAI systemic risk, US reporting thresholds), making Korea a datapoint for compute-triggered evaluation obligations.

### Gaps
- No official English translation of the AI Basic Act or its Enforcement Decree was located; all statutory details above are via law-firm/news summaries. The Korean official text (law.go.kr) was not consulted.
- The Korea AISI site (aisi.re.kr) refused connections during this session, so its official assessment framework description is from search snippets only; its publications list could not be enumerated.
- Details of the NIA enforcement guidance documents (titles, URLs) and of any TTA government-backed certification scheme criteria were not found in English.
- The 20 July 2026 amendment's exact scope rests on one news source.

## China: TC260 AI Safety Governance Framework, CAC Interim Measures for Generative AI, and GB/T 45654 testing requirements

### Takeaway
China is the only jurisdiction of the four with mandatory pre-deployment gating: under the CAC's Interim Measures (effective 15 August 2023), generative AI services with public-opinion attributes or social mobilization capacity must pass a security assessment and complete algorithm filing before public launch, with the recommended national standard GB/T 45654-2025 (effective late 2025) supplying the concrete testing methodology (training-data and generated-content checks, assessment appendices) used in filing and evaluation work, all framed by TC260's AI Safety Governance Framework (v2.0, 15 September 2025) which adds risk grading and lifecycle-based safety guidance.

### Cited Findings

**CAC Interim Measures for the Management of Generative AI Services (生成式人工智能服务管理暂行办法)**
- The Interim Measures took effect 15 August 2023 (secondary/translation sources; the two main free English translations — China Law Translate and DigiChina — returned 403/404 to automated fetch in this session, so article details below come from legal summaries and the Library of Congress note) — [Library of Congress Global Legal Monitor](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2023-07-18/china-generative-ai-measures-finalized/); [Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_Measures_for_the_Management_of_Generative_AI_Services)
- Article 17: providers of generative AI services "with public opinion attributes or social mobilization capacity" must carry out a security assessment (per the State's relevant provisions — i.e., the 2018 security-assessment regime for internet information services with public opinion attributes) and perform algorithm filing, modification, and cancellation filing procedures under the Algorithmic Recommendation Provisions (secondary: legal summaries; translation wording varies — "public opinion properties or social mobilization capacity" in some renderings) — [Covington, Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/key-takeaways-from-chinas-finalized-generative-artificial-intelligence-measures/); [DWT](https://dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2023/07/china-issues-generative-ai-regulations)
- The April 2023 draft (Article 6) had required ALL providers to apply to CAC for a security assessment before offering services to the public; the final version narrowed this to services with public-opinion/social-mobilization attributes (secondary) — [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/key-takeaways-from-chinas-finalized-generative-artificial-intelligence-measures/)
- Free English translations exist from China Law Translate (dated 2023/07/13) and Digital Policy Alert (both are unofficial translations) — [China Law Translate](https://www.chinalawtranslate.com/en/generative-ai-interim/) (403 to automated fetch this session); [Digital Policy Alert translation](https://clairk-old.digitalpolicyalert.org/documents/china-interim-measures-for-the-management-of-generative-artificial-intelligence-services/raw)

**GB/T 45654-2025 — Basic Safety/Security Requirements for Generative AI Services**
- Full title (per CSET's English translation): "Cybersecurity Technology – Basic Safety Requirements for Generative Artificial Intelligence Services" (网络安全技术 生成式人工智能服务安全基本要求), GB/T 45654—2025, issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation and the Standardization Administration of China; original Chinese standard issued 30 June 2025; CSET's English translation published 28 May 2026. CSET translates 安全 mainly as "safety" because the standard focuses on preventing accidental/unforeseen problems (translation, clearly labeled) — [CSET translation page](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/china-gen-ai-safety-standard-final/); [translated PDF](https://cset.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/t0661_gen_ai_safety_standard_final_EN.pdf)
- Scope and function (per CSET translation): applies to providers of generative AI services; serves as a reference for oversight departments and third-party evaluation institutions; primarily targets services with public opinion–shaping or social mobilization capabilities; supports filing, registration, testing, and evaluation work. Appendix A lists major safety risks for training data and generated content; Appendix B gives reference methods for safety assessment — [CSET](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/china-gen-ai-safety-standard-final/)
- The standard was formulated based on TC260's February 2024 technical document on generative AI safety (TC260-003 lineage) and is meant to be used alongside GB/T 45652 (pre-training and fine-tuning data) and GB/T 45674 (data annotation) (translation + search summary) — [CSET](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/china-gen-ai-safety-standard-final/); [CSET PDF](https://cset.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/t0661_gen_ai_safety_standard_final_EN.pdf)
- The "GB/T" designation denotes a recommended (voluntary) national standard rather than a mandatory (GB) one; in practice it operationalizes the mandatory CAC filing/assessment regime. An effective date of 1 November 2025 is reported by secondary standards-watch sources, not confirmed on the CSET page (flagged) — [SESEC landscape of AI security standards](https://sesec.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/SESEC-V-Newsletter-Annex-II-Landscape-of-AI-Security-Standards-in-China.pdf)
- Specific numeric test parameters circulating online (e.g., a 5% training-data contamination threshold, a question bank with refusal-rate targets, keyword lists, 31 risk categories) appear in compliance-consultancy sources and were NOT verified against the translated standard text in this session — treat as unconfirmed until checked against the [CSET PDF](https://cset.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/t0661_gen_ai_safety_standard_final_EN.pdf)

**TC260 AI Safety Governance Framework**
- TC260 (National Technical Committee 260 on Cybersecurity, under SAC) released AI Safety Governance Framework 2.0 on 15 September 2025 during National Cybersecurity (Awareness) Week; it is published bilingually, with the English translation at the back of the Chinese PDF (secondary: SESEC summary, verified by direct fetch) — [SESEC](https://sesec.eu/?p=2036)
- Changes from the 2024 first edition (v1.0): adds a third risk category, "derivative risks from AI applications" (job displacement, resource/energy pressure, bias amplification); adds grading of AI risks (v1.0 graded only AI systems); risk grading considers application scenario, level of intelligence, and application scale, with five levels — low, moderate, considerable, major, extremely serious; replaces stakeholder-based guidelines with lifecycle-based guidance (R&D, deployment, operation, use), retaining the overall structure of governance principles, safety risks, technical measures, and safety guidelines (secondary) — [SESEC](https://sesec.eu/?p=2036); [Geopolitechs analysis](https://www.geopolitechs.org/p/china-releases-upgraded-ai-safety)
- The official bilingual v2.0 document is attached as an appendix to the CAC's release announcement, reported at https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-09/15/c_1759653448369123.htm (URL via secondary source; not directly fetched/verified this session) — [Geopolitechs](https://www.geopolitechs.org/p/china-releases-upgraded-ai-safety)
- The framework sets the governance architecture that individual standards (like GB/T 45654) implement; supporting national/sector standards, e.g., on classification and grading methods for AI applications, are in development (secondary) — [SESEC](https://sesec.eu/?p=2036); [TechJack overview of TC260 standards (consultancy — low reliability for specifics)](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-governance-china/tc260-standards/)

### Inferences
- China's evaluation regime is the inverse of Singapore's and Japan's: evaluation evidence (data-source audits, generated-content testing, assessment reports under GB/T 45654 Appendix B) is produced to satisfy a mandatory pre-launch gate (security assessment + algorithm/model filing), not as voluntary assurance. For the Atlas this is the key teaching contrast: same technical activities (benchmarks, refusal testing, data checks), different institutional function (licensing-like gating vs. voluntary assurance).
- The TC260 framework's five-level risk grading by scenario/intelligence/scale (v2.0) parallels the risk-tiering logic of the EU AI Act but is implemented through standards and filing practice rather than statute.
