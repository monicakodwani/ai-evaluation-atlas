summary: >-
  The OECD's public observatory aggregating AI policies, indicators,
  incidents, and implementation tools across countries.
definition: >-
  OECD.AI collects and publishes comparable information about national AI
  policies, trends and indicators, and — through an associated incidents
  monitor described in the reports — reported AI incidents and hazards
  derived from public sources. It is an aggregation and comparison
  platform: it documents the AI landscape rather than testing systems.

strengths:
  - Cross-national comparability of policy and indicator data maintained by
    an intergovernmental body.
  - Continuous, public, and citable — useful for longitudinal accountability
    and policy research.
limitations:
  - Heterogeneous definitions across countries and sources limit
    comparability, per the reports.
  - Incident data derived from public reports inherits media-visibility and
    reporting bias.
  - Little direct system testing — the observatory documents the ecosystem
    rather than evaluating systems.
appropriate_claims:
  - Documented policies, initiatives, or reported incidents of a given kind
    exist across the covered jurisdictions.
  - Trends in reporting, policy activity, or indicators over time, as
    claims about the record.
inappropriate_claims:
  - That observatory data establishes the safety, effectiveness, or
    compliance of any particular AI system.
  - That cross-country indicator differences measure real differences in
    outcomes rather than differences in definitions and reporting.
why_it_matters: >-
  Policymakers compare countries using observatory data; conflating differences in reporting and definitions with real differences in outcomes misleads policy.
evidence_produced: >-
  Cross-national aggregation of AI policies, indicators, and reported
  incidents — comparative documentation of the policy landscape, not
  testing of systems.
combine_with:
  - Primary national sources before relying on any specific country claim.
  - System-level evaluation for any claim about actual systems.
  - Denominator and methodology checks before cross-country comparisons.
misconceptions:
  - misconception: Observatory indicators measure real national outcomes.
    reality: >-
      They aggregate what countries report under varying definitions —
      strong evidence about policy activity and reporting, weak evidence
      about outcomes on the ground.
questions_to_ask:
  - Are the definitions behind an indicator comparable across the
    countries being compared?
  - What is the source and lag of the data for each country?
  - Does the indicator measure policy activity or policy effect?
  - What would this observatory not see even if it were widespread?
